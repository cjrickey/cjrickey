**Primary audience:** Small/midsize GCs
**Topic:** What separates a useful OAC schedule update from a useless one

---

Most OAC schedule updates get read in about eight seconds.

The owner's rep skims for one thing — "are we still hitting the date?" — and if your update doesn't answer that in the first sentence, it gets filed under "the GC says everything's fine again."

The useless version is a wall of activity IDs and percentages. It's technically accurate and tells the room nothing. Nobody in that meeting is going to reconstruct the critical path from a bar chart in their head.

The useful version leads with the answer, then backs it up: what finished this period that mattered, what's driving the schedule right now, and what's coming next that could move the date. Named work, not activity codes. Plain language, not P6 jargon.

The problem is that writing the good version every single week takes real time — usually a scheduler's time, or an evening of yours. Which is why most updates quietly drift back toward the copy-paste version.
